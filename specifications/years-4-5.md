# PART A. TIMING AND PROGRESSION

## A1. Midpoint synchronization

Year 5 is the mandatory midpoint of the 90-minute workshop.

**At minute 45, every company must be brought to the Year 5 checkpoint.**

The facilitator controls a session-wide midpoint action.

When the midpoint is triggered:

1. Companies that have completed Year 5 retain their resolved decisions and outcomes.
2. Companies still in Years 1–4 are advanced through the remaining years using the predefined defaults.
3. All scheduled external and internal events for those years are resolved in chronological order.
4. Companies currently discussing a decision receive a warning and a short final submission window before the facilitator advances them.
5. Every company receives its Year 5 scenario and must resolve its Year 5 decision.
6. Every active student completes the Year 5 individual reflection.
7. Companies wait until all active team members have submitted.
8. The whole-class checkpoint becomes available once every company is ready.
9. The facilitator reveals the industry results and releases the second half of the simulation.

The 45-minute mark is a synchronization target. The facilitator controls the final release of the checkpoint.

**Important:** The facilitator must be able to initiate midpoint synchronization before minute 45 if necessary.

## A2. Year 4

Optional year. Five-minute decision.

If skipped, apply the default and events automatically.

No written reflection.

## A3. Year 5

Mandatory core year.

Every company must experience the Year 5 external event, make a Year 5 decision, receive its consequences, and complete the reflection checkpoint.

Year 5 cannot be silently skipped by midpoint synchronization.

### B3. Private intelligence: Before decision

**CFO**

Reservations delay incoming cash and leave some tickets temporarily unavailable. Rewards create future redemption obligations that must be funded.

**CTO**

VFI can help link purchases across accounts. Without it, some forms of transfer and reward abuse may be harder to detect.

**Marketing & PR**

Frequent concertgoers are enthusiastic about rewards. Occasional customers may benefit more from flexible payments and simpler ticket transfers.

**Legal**

Transfer restrictions and loyalty terms must be clear. Customers may challenge lost points, cancelled reservations or refused transfers.

**Employee Representative**

Refunds and account disputes already generate substantial support work. New features will require training and may increase workload.

**Sustainability**

Ticket transfers can reduce unused tickets. Rewards may encourage additional consumption and travel.

**Artist & Venue Relations**

Artists favour affordable resale. Promoters are interested in rewards that encourage repeat attendance and premium purchases.
