Implement in the existing FairPass application.

Year 9 and Year 10 are mandatory core years.

Year 10 concludes with:

# PART A. FINAL SESSION STRUCTURE

## A1. Timing

The full workshop lasts approximately 90 minutes.

The Year 5 checkpoint occurs around minute 45.

Suggested second-half structure:

- Minutes 45–48: Facilitator releases Year 6.
- Minutes 48–60: Years 6–8, including optional-year skipping.
- Minutes 60–69: Year 9 strategic decision.
- Minutes 69–78: Year 10 accountability decision and closing reflection.
- Minutes 78–83: Final industry checkpoint.
- Minutes 83–88: Facilitator debrief.
- Minutes 88–90: Student feedback and report access.

These are facilitator targets, not automatic countdown requirements.

The facilitator can adjust pacing.

## A2. Final synchronization

The facilitator has a session-wide control:

`Begin final checkpoint`

When activated:

1. Companies that completed Year 10 retain their resolved decisions and outcomes.
2. Companies in optional Years 6 or 8 complete those years through their defaults.
3. Companies in mandatory Years 7, 9 or 10 receive a final submission window.
4. When that window expires, unresolved company decisions use their defined expired-decision defaults.
5. All remaining events and state transitions resolve chronologically.
6. Every company reaches the Year 10 closing reflection.
7. Every active student submits the closing reflection.
8. Companies wait at the final checkpoint.
9. The facilitator releases the industry results simultaneously.

No company may bypass the Year 10 reflection.

A facilitator may mark an absent or disconnected participant inactive.

An inactive participant's missing response does not block the session.

## A3. Mandatory years

Year 9: Core strategic decision.

Year 10: Core accountability decision and final reflection.

Expired decisions use their specified defaults.

Do not record expired defaults as student-selected choices.

## A4. Decision resolution order

For each year:

1. Resolve start-of-year financial and contractual transitions.
2. Present the fixed external event.
3. Present the company scenario.
4. Deliver private role intelligence.
5. Collect individual preferred options.
6. Resolve executive powers and company decision.
7. Apply decision costs and immediate effects.
8. Resolve conditional consequences.
9. Select and resolve one internal event.
10. Display the actual outcome.
11. Deliver private consequences.
12. Update company state and history.
13. Advance or enter the closing reflection.

The company decision and each student's personal preference remain separate records.

# PART B. FINANCIAL AND STATE CONTINUITY

## B1. Starting Year 9

Carry forward all company state from Year 8.

At the beginning of Year 9:

- Charge annual VFI operating costs if operational.
- Recognize active Northstar revenue.
- Recognize active venue-entry contract revenue.
- Recognize active Meridian partnership revenue.
- Recognize active independent programme revenue.
- Preserve outstanding obligations.
- Preserve all historical events.
- Preserve the Year 5 checkpoint snapshot.

Annual baseline revenue remains €24M unless explicitly changed.

## B2. Starting Year 10

Apply the same annual operating and contract-recognition rules.

Do not charge previous investment costs again.

No additional routine expenses are inferred.

## B3. Revenue

Reported annual revenue is calculated as:

`baseline_revenue + active_contract_revenue + active_programme_revenue`

Each contract or programme contributes once.

Inactive, conditional, expired, or unsuccessful agreements contribute zero.

Do not add revenue directly to cash.

## B4. Cash

Cash changes only through explicitly defined transactions.

Each transaction uses an idempotent ledger entry.

Never allow negative cash.

If annual VFI operating costs exceed available cash:

1. Charge the available cash up to the amount owed.
2. Record the unpaid remainder as `vfi_operating_payable_m`.
3. Set cash to zero.
4. Increase financial pressure by one.
5. Do not automatically terminate VFI.
6. Do not charge the same unpaid amount twice.

At the next annual financial transition, pay existing operating payables first, to the extent cash is available.

Do not silently forgive unpaid liabilities.

This rule also applies to any previously recorded unpaid recurring operating obligation.

## B5. Financial distress

A company with cash below €1M is financially vulnerable.

A company with cash equal to zero is in liquidity distress.

Neither condition automatically causes bankruptcy, layoffs, or liquidation.

The company must address the situation through an explicit decision or carry it into the final report.

## B6. Existing unresolved obligations

Preserve:

- Restructuring proposals.
- Verification remediation.
- Support training.
- Integration governance.
- Independent review remediation.
- Customer compensation review.
- Staff escalation review.
- Data-handling review.
- Commercial contract review.
- Partnership conditions disputes.
- Pending independent programmes.
- Other previously established unresolved obligations.

Only explicitly defined decisions resolve obligations.

Your board has called an extraordinary strategy meeting.

The board wants a strategy for the next five years.

**What kind of company will you become?**

| Option | Strategy | Immediate cost |
|---|---|---:|

| C | Restructure around a leaner operating model | €200K |

## C3. Private intelligence

**CEO**

The board expects a defensible long-term strategy. Directors want to know which earlier commitments the company intends to preserve and which it is prepared to change.

**CFO**

Cash reserves and recurring obligations differ substantially between companies. Expansion may increase revenue, but financial distress could limit the company's ability to meet existing commitments.

**CTO**

Your technical architecture now shapes what the company can realistically offer. Some systems are expensive to maintain, while others create opportunities that competitors cannot easily reproduce.

**Marketing & PR**

Customer trust reflects years of service experiences, controversies, and public commitments. A major strategic change could attract new audiences while alienating existing ones.

**Head of Legal**

Unresolved contractual, customer, employment, and data obligations remain enforceable. A strategic reorganization does not automatically remove them.

**Employee Representative**

Employees want to know whether the company intends to invest in their capabilities, change working conditions, or reduce staffing. Several teams are already under pressure.

**Head of Sustainability**

The company's long-term impact depends on infrastructure, access, employment, and relationships with local communities. Future growth may increase both benefits and burdens.

**Head of Artist & Venue Relations**

Major promoters offer scale, while independent artists value flexibility. Your existing relationships will determine which strategic promises are credible.

# PART J. FINAL STATE AND OUTCOME RULES

## J1. No artificial ending

Do not automatically bankrupt or rescue companies at the end.

A company may finish with:

- Strong reserves.
- Weak reserves.
- High or low customer trust.
- Strong or weak workforce conditions.
- Active contracts.
- Unresolved obligations.
- Commercial opportunities.
- Strategic dependencies.

These are valid endings.

## J2. No universal winner

The facilitator dashboard may show leaders for individual indicators.

Do not produce:

- Overall ethics score.
- Overall responsibility score.
- Universal best company.
- Single moral ranking.

## J3. Mission outcomes

Evaluate role missions using actual state and documented events.

For the CEO's mission to meet at least three of four board targets, use the four board targets already defined in the general simulation configuration.

Do not invent new targets.

If the configuration lacks those targets, mark the CEO mission evaluation as unavailable and preserve the underlying performance data.

For other roles, use their established mission criteria.

## J4. Executive powers

Executive powers remain usable only once per student across the decade.

A power used earlier cannot be reused.

Year 9 and Year 10 must respect the same power rules as earlier years.

Do not create additional executive powers.

## J5. Closing the session

After the facilitator releases the final checkpoint:

- Mark the simulation complete.
- Lock company decisions.
- Preserve all records.
- Allow individual report access.
- Allow voluntary feedback.
- Allow facilitator export of aggregate company results.
- Prevent additional financial or state transitions.

Do not reset the simulation automatically.
